gss16_advfront <- gss16 %>%

Re-levelling advfront

gss16_advfront <- gss16_advfront %>%
  mutate(
    advfront = case_when(
      advfront == "Strongly agree" ~ "Agree",
      advfront == "Agree" ~ "Agree",
      TRUE ~ "Not agree"
    ),
    advfront = fct_relevel(advfront, "Not agree", "Agree")
  )

Re-levelling polviews

gss16_advfront <- gss16_advfront %>%
  mutate(
    polviews = case_when(
      str_detect(polviews, "[Cc]onservative") ~ "Conservative",
      str_detect(polviews, "[Ll]iberal") ~ "Liberal",
      TRUE ~ polviews
    ),
    polviews = fct_relevel(polviews, "Conservative", "Moderate", "Liberal")
  )

The formula for the line-of-best-fit is emailhr = -2.757 + 0.685 x educ. The interpretation of this estimate is that for each extra year of education level a person has, the number of hours they spend on email in a week increases by 0.685 hours. The intercept does not have a meaningful interpretation because someone cannot spend -2.757 hours on email.

gss16_wflow_1 <- workflow () %>%
  add_model(gss16_mod_1) %>%
  add_recipe(gss16_rec_1)

Exercise 2 (b)

I chose a logistic regression for this model because we are using the numerical predictor educ to predict the binary categorical outcome of advfront, which is either “Agree” or “Not Agree”. Since we are predicting this binary outcome, it does not make sense to use a different type of regression such as a linear model. The logistic regression is also most appropriate for the “glm” computational engine I use in this model.

After having calculated the sensitivity, specificity, and area under the ROC curves for both models, it is safe to say that model 1 shows better performance. Model 1 has a specificity of 0.643, whereas model 2 has a specificity of 0.393. This shows that model 1 has a much lower false negative rate than model 2, which shows that its predictions are more accurate. Moreover, looking at the ROC curves, the AUC for model 1 is 0.581 and the AUC for model 2 is 0.535. When looking at the ROC, the AUC should be as close to 1 as possible, since that indicates better model performance and higher accuracy in making predictions. The only statistic that acts as a weakness for model 1 is its lower sensitivity (0.555) in comparison to model 2 (0.668). However, the difference is minor and likely does not take any from model 1’s accuracy.
